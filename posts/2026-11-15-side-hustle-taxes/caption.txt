Got a side hustle? Detailing cars, mowing lawns, reselling sneakers, tutoring? Here's the tax rule most teens miss.

If your net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more, you owe self-employment tax (IRS). Net earnings means what you made minus business costs like supplies. The tax is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare. At a job your employer pays half; self-employed, you pay both. Example: $3,000 of profit means about $424 in self-employment tax (figured on 92.35% of profit).

New for 2026: businesses only have to send you a 1099-NEC once they pay you $2,000 or more, and payment apps send a 1099-K after $20,000 and 200 transactions. The income is still taxable either way, so track every payment and expense.

Data: IRS self-employment tax page (updated Oct. 30, 2026); 1099 changes in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (CPA Practice Advisor).
