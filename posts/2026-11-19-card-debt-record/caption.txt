Americans owe a record $1.29 trillion on credit cards, up $52 billion from a year earlier (New York Fed, Q3 2026). Total household debt hit $19.1 trillion, up $334 billion in just three months.

Most of that is mortgages ($13.4 trillion), then auto loans ($1.73 trillion), student loans ($1.66 trillion) and credit cards ($1.29 trillion). 5.0% of all household debt was in some stage of delinquency.

Card debt is the expensive kind: cards that charged interest averaged a 22.36% APR in Q3 2026 (Federal Reserve). Paying your full statement balance each month means you generally pay no interest on purchases.

Data: New York Fed Household Debt and Credit Report (Nov. 10, 2026); Federal Reserve G.19 (Nov. 6, 2026); CFPB.
