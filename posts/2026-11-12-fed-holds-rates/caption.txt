The Federal Reserve kept its key interest rate at 3.75% to 4% at its Oct. 28 meeting. The vote was 10 to 2, and both officials who disagreed wanted to raise rates by a quarter point.

Why hold instead of cutting? The Fed's own statement says inflation "remains elevated." Prices were up 3.7% over the year to September (the latest 12-month reading), well above the Fed's 2% goal.

What it means for you: when the Fed's rate stays high, credit card and loan rates tend to stay high too, and savings accounts can keep paying more. Pay cards off in full and shop around for a better savings rate.

Data: Federal Reserve FOMC statement (Oct. 28, 2026); BLS CPI for September 2026.
